# If you already know the original

> **DRAFT — DO NOT USE.**

The number cards are the part you do not relearn. Four suits, ranks 1 through 14, one trump suit, follow suit, and the 14s pay a bonus when you take them. A plain 14 pays 10. The trump 14 pays 20. You relearn the names of the people, and a handful of sentences about scoring and the optional cards.

Play the first watch with the [player aid](player-aid.md) in reach.

## The night is the same shape

1. Deal a small hand. The hand grows as the watches pass.
2. Everyone writes a number from 0 through the number of cards, in secret. Reveal together.
3. The player left of the dealer leads. The winner of a trick leads the next one.
4. Follow the led suit when you have it. A special card may always be played. A numbered card of another suit may not, and that includes the trump suit.
5. The first card that is not a Cloak sets the led suit, and only if that card has a suit. A person, the Squire, Truce, or Smoke in that spot means the trick has no led suit.
6. Steel beats the other suits. Higher rank of the same suit beats lower rank, up to 14. A different plain suit does not beat the led suit.
7. Specials beat numbers. Equals: the one played earlier wins. If every card is a Cloak, the first one wins.
8. One card is declared as you play it. Armed, it stands with the Knights. Cloaked, it is a blank.
9. There is a three-step cycle. A Fool beats the Count. The Count beats the Knights. A Knight beats a Fool. If all three roles are in one trick, the Fool wins, and only the Fool's bonus is scored.
10. Bonuses come from the 14s you take and from that cycle. A true bid of zero is the dramatic bid in the sharp scoring mode. There is also an even scoring mode where every exact bid is worth the same pot, and being off by one is worth half.

## Say these four sentences before you deal

1. **Stake** pays `15 × bid` for a made bid of 1 or more, and `8 × cards` for a made zero. A miss of a bid of 1 or more costs `6 × how many you were off`. A broken zero costs `8 × cards`, whether you took one trick or all of them. On a 10-card watch a made zero is 80: that beats a made bid of 5 and loses to a made bid of 6.
2. **Stake pays bonuses only when the bid is true.** Glide is the mode that halves them. In Glide, add every bonus into one total first, then drop the fraction. Off by two or more, that total is gone. The 14s are the familiar ones: 10 for a plain Tithe, 20 for the Steel Tithe.
3. **Glide** has no special zero and no negative scores. Everyone's pot is `8 × cards`. Exact takes the pot. Off by one takes half. Off by two or more takes nothing.
4. **There is no side bet.** None of the Knights wagers points on the bid.

## Four tricks that will surprise you

**Smoke keeps the led suit.** Trump is off and specials cannot win. A led Wool 6 beats a Steel 14. Smoke is not "highest number in the trick." If the trick had no led suit, then the highest rank wins, and a tie goes to the card played earlier.

**Truce's next lead is the player after the Truce**, clockwise, even when someone else would have won. The would-be winner does not lead.

**Truce and Smoke in the same trick cancel.** Treat both as Cloaks and resolve the rest normally. The later one does not replace the earlier one.

**The Oath pays only if neither of you missed by two or more.** You do not both have to be exact. If either of you is that far off, neither of you scores the 10. A miss of one still lets the other player be paid. If you played the Oath and you also won that trick, there is no pact.

## Powers, in one pass

They fire only when that Knight wins the trick.

- **Marshal** names who leads next. On the last trick, skip it.
- **Steward** secretly reorders the top three of the undealt pile. The Steward does not take them. On the last trick, or if the pile is empty, skip it.
- **Exchanger** may draw one and discard one face down, out of the game. Only when a card remains in your hand and the pile is not empty.
- **Almoner** may give the trick to a player who is still short of their bid. They take the count, the bonuses, and the next lead. A clean zero is not short, and a bid that has already been met is not short.

The Squire has no power. Armed, the Squire is a Knight for the ladder and the bonuses. Cloaked, the Squire is a blank.

## What you can stop tracking

The numbered cards run from 1 to 14, and a full game deals at most 1 through 10. There are four Cloaks, four Knights, one Squire, one Count, and two Fools. The base deck has 68 cards. Two to six players. No fifth fighter, no wager, no second alliance card.

## First-watch script

Use Stake, the base deck, and the Half watch (hands of 3, 4, 5, 6, 5, 4). Speak the new words even when your hand wants the old ones. After that watch, the [rules](rules.md) are only for arguments. The arithmetic for the pad is in [scoring](scoring.md).
